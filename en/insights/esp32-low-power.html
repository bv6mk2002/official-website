Low-power ESP32 design for battery products: deep sleep in practice

The ESP32 chip can draw only microamps in deep sleep, yet many products measure several milliamps. The cause is usually the surrounding circuitry, not the chip itself.

Contents
  1. 1. A development board is not a product
  2. 2. Choose the right wake-up source
  3. 3. Wi-Fi is the biggest consumer
  4. 4. Estimating battery life

With built-in Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, the ESP32 is a popular choice for IoT products. According to the datasheet, the chip can draw around 10 µA in deep sleep. Yet many people measure several milliamps on a development board and find their batteries last only a few weeks. Here is what we have learned designing battery-powered products.

1. A development board is not a product

The USB-to-UART bridge, power LED and general-purpose LDO on typical development boards all draw current continuously. For a product you should:

  • Choose an LDO or DC-DC converter with low quiescent current (Iq), ideally a few µA.
  • Remove the power LED and USB bridge, or make them switchable.
  • Switch off sensors, external memory and other peripherals with a MOSFET or load switch during sleep.
  • Check pull-up / pull-down resistors and voltage dividers for constant leakage paths.

2. Choose the right wake-up source

The ESP32 can wake from deep sleep in several ways:

  • RTC timer: wake periodically to measure and report — the most common.
  • External GPIO (ext0 / ext1): buttons, door contacts, sensor interrupts.
  • Touch pins: a user interface without mechanical buttons.
  • ULP coprocessor: takes simple measurements while the main cores sleep and wakes them only when a threshold is reached.

Variables you need to keep can live in RTC memory (e.g. RTC_DATA_ATTR), so you don't have to re-initialize everything after waking.

3. Wi-Fi is the biggest consumer

Connecting and transmitting over Wi-Fi causes current peaks of several hundred mA. Shortening the time the device is awake makes a bigger difference than lowering sleep current:

  • Use a static IP and remember the AP channel and BSSID to connect faster.
  • Batch several readings into one transmission.
  • Add enough bulk capacitance so current peaks do not cause brown-outs and resets.
  • For long range and small data, consider a lower-power radio such as LoRa.

4. Estimating battery life

Start with the average current:

Average current = (active current × active time + sleep current × sleep time) ÷ period

For example, waking every 10 minutes, 3 s active at an average of 80 mA, and 20 µA in sleep:

(80 mA × 3 s + 0.02 mA × 597 s) ÷ 600 s ≈ 0.42 mA
2000 mAh ÷ 0.42 mA ≈ 4,760 h ≈ 6.5 months (excluding self-discharge and temperature effects)

The active time dominates the average. Cutting it from 3 s to 1 s nearly triples battery life.

Measure to be sure: use a power analyzer with a dynamic range from µA to hundreds of mA, record the full wake-up cycle, and optimize the firmware based on the waveform.
